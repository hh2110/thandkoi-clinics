Privacy

This site is the public face of a clinic, so it is built to need almost nothing from you. There is no account to make, no form to fill in, and no cookie set on your browser. This page says exactly what does happen, including the parts that are less comfortable to write down.

It is written for a person to read rather than for a lawyer, and every statement on it was checked against the code that runs this site — not copied from a template. Last updated 16 August 2026.

This notice is in English only for now. If you would like any of it explained in Urdu or Pashto, please ask us using the contact details at the end — we would rather explain it properly than publish a translation nobody has checked.

Who is responsible

The Thandkoi Clinics — a not-for-profit, family-run primary care clinic in Thandkoi, Swabi (Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan) — decides what this site is for and what it publishes, and is responsible for the clinic's own patient records and for anything you send to the clinic directly.

North West Data Products Ltd built this website and the software behind it, and runs the hosting on the clinic's behalf. It is a company registered in England and Wales, company number 17388764, registered office Unit A, 82 James Carter Road, Mildenhall, Bury St. Edmunds, IP28 7DE. For the clinic's data it acts on the clinic's instructions. For two narrow things — the visitor statistics and the error diagnostics described below — it decided to collect them, for its own purpose of keeping the site running affordably, so it answers for those itself.

In plain terms: questions about the clinic, its patients or its funding go to the clinic; questions about the website's own analytics or error reports go to privacy@northwestdata.io. Either address will pass your question on if it arrives in the wrong place.

What we collect from you when you visit

Nothing that you type, because there is nothing to type into. This site has no contact form, no sign-up, no comments, no search box that we record, and no payment page. The only form anywhere in this software is the one clinic staff use to upload their daily export, and that sits behind a login.

No cookies are set on your browser by this site. That is why you have never seen a cookie banner here — there is nothing to ask you about. Signed-in staff get a session cookie in the admin area; an ordinary visitor gets none.

If you switch the site between light and dark, that choice is remembered inside your browser and is never sent to us. It is why the setting follows the device rather than you.

This site keeps no access log of its own: the web server runs without one, so there is no file here recording which address requested which page.

The three outside services involved

Three, and no more. They are all listed here, including what each one can see.

  • Umami Cloud counts visits. It is a deliberately minimal, cookieless analytics tool: it sets nothing on your browser and it never identifies anyone. What we look at is visitors, visits, page views, how long a visit lasted, which pages were read and which site sent someone here. There is no way for us to go from any of that to a person, and we do not try.
  • Sentry tells us when the site breaks. A report is sent only when something goes wrong, and it carries the address of the page and the technical detail of the fault, plus the name and version your browser gives out. It is configured so that it carries no IP address, no cookies, and none of the contents of any form or upload — that last one matters here, because an upload on this site is a file of patient records. Those reports are stored in Sentry's European region.
  • Cloudflare stores and serves the photographs on this site, from media.thandkoiclinics.com. Loading a page with photos on it means your browser fetches them from there.

There is one embedded item on the whole site: the map on the Contact page comes from Google Maps. Unlike everything above, Google can see your address and can set its own cookies inside that map frame, under its own terms rather than ours. It is the only place on this site where that happens.

Beyond those: no advertising, no ad networks, no social-media tracking pixels, no session recording, and no fonts loaded from anyone else's servers — the typefaces are served from this site itself. The links to our Facebook and Instagram pages are ordinary links; nothing is shared with either company unless you click one.

Giving Zakat or Sadaqa

The Donate page is a page of information, not a checkout. It shows the clinic's bank details and how to get in touch; there is no card form, no payment processor and no donation button anywhere on this site. If you make a transfer, that happens between you, your bank and the clinic's bank — this website plays no part in it and is told nothing about it.

If you write or call about giving, or about giving medicine, equipment or your time, your message goes to the clinic's own inbox or phone and stays there. It is not stored in this website, and it is not passed to anyone else.

Patient information, and what this site does with it

The clinic's own software holds full patient records. Once a day, staff upload an export of the day's activity so the site can publish figures about the clinic's work. That export contains real patient detail, so this is the part of the system built most carefully — and it is worth being precise about it rather than reassuring.

What is thrown away, and when

The uploaded file is read in memory, during the upload, and is never written to disk or to the database. Nothing keeps a copy of it. Of what it contains:

  • Patient name, father's or husband's name, address, medical record number and all vital signs are never read at all. The software has no instruction anywhere to look at those columns.
  • Date of birth is read once, to work out which age band the patient falls into, and then discarded. Only the band is kept — one of 0–5, 6–18, 19–55 or 56+. The date itself is never stored.

What is kept

One record per visit, with no name and no way to reach a person through it:

  • the date of the visit, and the department;
  • the age band, and whether the patient was recorded as male, female, or neither/not recorded;
  • a rough location — a village or union council, never a street address;
  • a diagnosis category picked from a fixed list of ten, rather than what was written;
  • whether it was a first visit or a follow-up;
  • whether the visit was covered by Zakat or paid for;
  • the fees for registration, consultation, pharmacy, laboratory and ultrasound.

The part that deserves plain language

Alongside those, seven columns of the clinician's own writing are kept: presenting complaints, investigation, the written diagnosis, prescribed medicine, the doctor's, nurse's or dietitian's notes, diet and drug compliance, and the plan. That is more than the word "de-identified" might suggest on its own, so here is exactly where it stands.

  • They are kept because the clinic's data-entry software cannot accept a patient's name or identifier into those particular boxes — they contain clinical description and nothing that points to a person.
  • None of them is ever shown on any public page. Not in part, not in summary form, not anywhere.
  • They are used for one thing: an automatically written paragraph describing the day's clinical pattern in general terms, grouped only as adult men, adult women, and children.
  • That paragraph is only published for a group of at least three patients. Below three it is not written and not shown, so a single day's single patient can never become a sentence about them. This rule is enforced in code, not by asking the writing tool nicely — because a previous version relied on the latter and it did not hold.

Automatic writing, and what it is given

Some prose on this site — a summary sentence on a daily report, and the monthly newsletter draft — is written by an AI model run by Anthropic. Two rules govern it, and both are enforced in code:

  • It is never given a patient record. It receives counts, categories and the clinical text described above, grouped into those three buckets. No name, no identifier, no date of birth, no exact age, no location.
  • Every number you see was calculated here, not by the model. The model writes sentences around figures that were already worked out; it is never the source of a statistic.

The monthly newsletter is a draft until a person reads and publishes it.

Where the figures appear

Daily and monthly report pages publish counts and percentages — how many visits, the split by age and sex, how many were Zakat-supported. Nothing on them identifies anyone. The daily pages additionally ask search engines not to keep a copy, so their content does not settle permanently into search results and web archives.

How long things are kept

The uploaded export lasts only as long as the upload itself. The visit-level records above have no deletion date: they are what every published figure is recalculated from, so a year of them is what makes a yearly total possible. They contain no identity to delete.

Visitor statistics and error reports are kept for as long as Umami and Sentry hold them under their own retention settings.

Asking us something, or asking us to stop

If you have been treated at the clinic and want to know what is held about you, ask the clinic — its own records are the ones that identify you, and this website does not hold them. If you want a figure corrected or a page taken down, tell us and we will look at it.
